Shaped by the community

UX challenge

Little data, so the community became the source of truth. Surveys and interviews with runners and local businesses shaped what loyalty should offer — and what partners could support.

Research scope

Surveys
30 athletes · mixed levels
Interviews
5 local business owners
Methods
Google Forms · in-person interviews

Needs and pains

Health & balance

Need Stay active and recover from work stress.

Pain Little energy or time outside training.

Progress & consistency

Need Visible goals and proof of improvement.

Pain Hard to stay consistent without feedback.

Community

Need Shared motivation and belonging.

Pain Solo training feels isolating.

Rewards

Need Tangible perks — discounts, wellness, event entries.

Pain Generic loyalty offers feel forgettable.

Motivation over time

Need Fresh goals and reasons to keep showing up.

Pain Routine turns into boredom; momentum fades.

Personas

The Dedicated Runner · Intermediate to advanced

Goal Stay engaged between races.

Needs Meaningful challenges and rewards that match effort — e.g. a free coffee after a long run.

The Newcomer · Beginner

Goal Build a habit and confidence.

Needs Simple onboarding, clear milestones, and low-pressure comparison with peers at a similar level.

The Local Business Owner · Partner

Goal Bring new customers through the door.

Needs A light way to join the program — perks that are easy to honor and worth offering.

The Cashier · Staff

Goal Keep checkout fast.

Needs Quick reward verification — no friction at the counter.

Customer journey map

01 · Awareness

Action Hears about the club or sees a local campaign.

Need Understand why it is worth joining.

Pain Unclear value or low trust.

Opportunity Lead with health, progress, and local rewards.

02 · Onboarding

Action Signs up and links activity tracking.

Need Fast setup and an early win.

Pain Long forms or unclear next steps.

Opportunity Guided first challenge and welcome perk.

03 · Engagement

Action Logs runs, joins challenges, checks progress.

Need Motivation and a sense of community.

Pain Progress feels invisible; group activity is hard to find.

Opportunity Milestones, social challenges, local group runs.

04 · Rewards

Action Browses perks and local offers.

Need Rewards that feel worth the effort.

Pain Thin or generic catalog.

Opportunity Mix everyday perks (coffee, gear) with experiences (spa, races).

05 · Redemption

Action Redeems points or sends them to a friend.

Need Recognition without hassle.

Pain Confusing flows at checkout.

Opportunity QR redemption and clear confirmation.

06 · Retention

Action Reviews progress and sets new goals.

Need Reasons to return after the first reward.

Pain Motivation drops once novelty wears off.

Opportunity Seasonal challenges and streak rewards.

Core flows

Join & explore

Flow Create profile · Learn features · Browse rewards and challenges

Purpose Show value early and let users personalize the experience.

Earn & redeem

Flow Track activity · Collect points · Activate and redeem

Purpose Close the loop between effort and reward.

Share points

Flow Choose friend · Set amount · Confirm transfer

Purpose Strengthen social ties inside the club.

Challenges

Flow Pick challenge · Track progress · Complete and celebrate

Purpose Keep training active between events.

Usability testing & SEQ

Participants
5 per round · 2 prototype iterations
Tool
Maze · Figma prototype
Task 1

Find a reward you cannot afford yet, see how to earn the missing points, and join a matching challenge.

Task 2

Activate a reward or redeem one already on your loyalty card.

Task 3

Send a chosen number of points to a specific friend.

Findings & changes

Core flows and key screens. For extended flows or a walkthrough — get in touch.

What stayed with me

Paused before launch, but the process still matters. Talks and prototype tests showed what runners and partners needed: local rewards, easy redemption, and competition that does not push beginners away.
